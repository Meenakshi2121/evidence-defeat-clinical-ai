# Development and independent-validation amendment v0.2

**Status:** Operational for transparent development; not registered or externally reviewed

**Date:** 14 October 2026

**Applies to:** Protocol v0.1 and the computational plan from Stage C onward

**Machine-readable specification:** `config/development_validation.yaml`

## 1. Reason for this amendment

Protocol v0.1 treated the proposed four-check gate as if it were already the
final method and reserved nearly all scientific output for a confirmatory run.
That is unnecessarily restrictive for a method-development study. The purpose
of the project is to construct a useful evidence-defeat framework, and careful
iteration is legitimate before the framework is frozen.

The methodological risk is not iteration itself. The risk is using the same
results both to modify the framework and to claim that the modified framework
has been independently validated. This amendment therefore separates visible,
auditable development from one-time validation on untouched evidence.

No scientific method comparison generated by the present repository had been
unblinded when this decision was made. Stage A exposed only invariant status,
runtime, memory and reproducibility fingerprints. Stage B has the same
disclosure restriction. RCT Bench patient-level files and real trial outcomes
have not been opened. The change therefore precedes scientific unblinding and
does not respond to a favourable or unfavourable result from the new pipeline.
The amendment now authorises Stage C scientific unblinding; the evidence-state
flag must be changed from `false` to `true` immediately after the first
successful development report is inspected.

## 2. What remains unchanged

Stages A and B remain diagnostic exercises. They establish that the software
runs, preserves fold isolation, matches coverage correctly and is
computationally feasible. Their reports are not eligible for candidate
selection, effect estimates or paper conclusions.

The primary validation estimand remains:

```text
Delta_UA = UA_complete - UA_uncertainty_matched
```

where material unsafe acceptance uses the whole-population denominator and the
uncertainty comparator has exactly matched coverage. A validation claim of
lower unsafe acceptance requires the upper limit of the two-sided 95%
confidence interval for `Delta_UA` to be below zero. Policy utility, regret,
coverage and group-specific results must be reported alongside this contrast;
a favourable safety contrast alone will not justify a deployment claim if
material harms are evident elsewhere.

The fairness analysis remains an audit rather than a guarantee. No weighted
fairness score will determine the conclusion.

## 3. Revised sequence

### 3.1 Stage C: transparent development

After diagnostic feasibility is complete, scientific outputs may be examined
only on a dedicated controlled-simulation development bank. This bank must
have its own scenario identifiers and replicate namespace and must be reviewed
and committed before its first run. Development bank v0.1 and baseline
candidate `C000` now meet this internal pre-run requirement; external review is
still required before the final development freeze.

Development may alter check definitions, logical architecture, thresholds,
learners, the population default and implementation details. It may not use
the reserved validation grid, RCT Bench patient-level files or real trial
outcomes. Development results are exploratory method-building evidence and
cannot be described as confirmatory.

Every candidate must be logged before it is run. The log records its candidate
and parent identifiers, the reason for the change, the Git commit,
configuration hashes, development-bank version and intended decision rule.
After the run, the complete required metric profile is appended. Unsuccessful
or inconvenient candidates remain in the repository's development record.

Candidate selection will not maximise a weighted composite. Review will
consider, separately, unsafe acceptance at matched coverage, policy utility,
regret, access to personalisation, raw and evidence-standardised group
differences, stability and complexity. Pareto-nondominated candidates are
preferred; simplicity breaks practical ties. The final choice and trade-offs
require a written rationale and supervisor/statistical review.

### 3.2 Stage D: development freeze

One final candidate is frozen by identifier, clean Git commit and complete
configuration hashes. The candidate history and selection rationale are
closed, the protocol is updated, reviewed and registered, and the repository
is tagged `protocol-v1.0`. No validation data are opened before this point.

### 3.3 Stage E: blinded variance pilot

The existing variance-pilot idea moves after the development freeze. It may
reveal only the standard deviation of the paired primary contrast and
computational resource use. It may change Monte Carlo replicate counts or
batching, but not the framework, thresholds, learner set, scenario membership
or primary estimand. Mean effects, rankings and group differences remain
concealed.

### 3.4 Stage F: one-time independent validation

The frozen candidate is evaluated once on the reserved validation simulation
bank and, subject to eligibility and governance checks, the unopened
RCT-shaped semi-synthetic and real-outcome multitrial layers. All prespecified
results are reported regardless of direction.

If validation results motivate a method change, that change defines a new
framework version. The original validation result remains reported, and the
new version requires new independent evidence; it cannot be re-tested on the
same validation results and called independently validated.

## 4. Interpretation of evidence

The evidence hierarchy after this amendment is:

| Evidence | Permitted use | Prohibited claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage A/B diagnostics | Software and feasibility decisions | Framework effectiveness |
| Stage C development | Improve and select a candidate | Independent validation |
| Stage E blinded pilot | Replicate count and compute planning | Method ranking or effect |
| Stage F validation | Test frozen claims | Retune the same version |

This design allows the framework to improve without disguising optimisation as
proof. A useful final method is the objective; an untouched validation phase is
what makes the eventual scientific claim credible.

## 5. Consequences for existing documents

Protocol v0.1 and computational plan v0.1 remain in the repository as the
historical pre-amendment record. This amendment supersedes their restrictions
on scientific inspection and method adaptation from Stage C onward. It does
not retrospectively change the status of any earlier output.
